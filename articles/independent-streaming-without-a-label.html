Independent streaming without a major label

Essay · Justin Helmer · excavationpro.ca

You can put music on the public internet without a label. That sentence is true and incomplete. The incomplete part is the work: encoding, naming, identifying, backing up, and refusing to confuse a dashboard with an audience.

I use a mix of a first-party player and third-party stores. The first-party player is for people who will tolerate a website. The stores are for people who will not. Neither is morally superior. They are different rooms.

A label, at its best, is logistics plus taste plus money. At its average, it is logistics you could have done plus taste you did not ask for plus money that arrives as a recoupable story. I am not anti-collaboration. I am anti-storytelling that pretends a contract is a personality.

Practical stack, the unromantic version:

  • Keep masters in a place you control, with checksums, not only in a laptop folder named “final_final2.”
  • Give public streams a stable URL and a human title.
  • Register ISRCs if you want the recording to be citable.
  • Write the license in language a tired person can read.
  • Mirror the player so one outage is not a disappearance.

None of that requires a major. All of that requires hours. Hours are the actual barrier, not a secret handshake. If a tool helps, a BPM detector, a catalog page, a listen hub, use it. If a tool wants your catalog as training data for a product you will never see, read twice.

This site is my proof that the writing can live next to the player. Other independent musicians should steal that structure, not the sentences. Steal structure. Write your own sentences. That is the whole ethic.

Metrics: I look at them, then I go back to the session. A spike without a song you still like is a sugar crash. A quiet month with three finished pieces is a harvest. I would rather harvest. Harvest does not trend. Harvest compounds.

If a distributor’s dashboard becomes the only place you “have” your music, you do not have your music. You have a login. Keep a copy you can play if the login dies. That is not paranoia. That is adult filing.

Why I give the music away

A storefront is not an archive

A streaming platform is a storefront. It can put a song in front of a listener, and that is useful. It is not the archive. The archive is the set of files, metadata, and rights records that survives a platform change. An artist who only has a storefront has a shop with no stockroom.

The direct room

A direct listening room is smaller and less algorithmic, and that is the point. The artist controls the page, the order, and the explanation. The listener does not need an account to hear the work. The trade is reach for control. For a catalog that is not chasing a playlist, that trade is worth making.

The two can coexist. The storefront can bring a new listener; the direct room can keep the record. The mistake is treating the storefront as the only copy and the algorithm as the only audience.

The listener's side

The listener does not care about the label structure. They care whether the page opens, whether the player works, and whether the artist is reachable. A direct room can answer all three without an account. That is the whole advantage.

The hard part is maintenance. A storefront is maintained for you. A direct room is maintained by the artist. The trade is control for work. If you want the control, budget the work.

The boring part

The boring part is the file naming, the backup, the metadata, and the link check. It is not a content strategy. It is the reason a listener can find the same track twice. An independent catalog without boring maintenance becomes a collection of broken promises with good artwork.

What a label actually provides

A label can provide capital, distribution, radio, and a team. It can also take ownership, decide the release date, and treat the artist as one project in a portfolio. An independent artist has to replace the useful parts without pretending the costs do not exist. The direct room replaces distribution and radio with a smaller, more controlled reach. It does not replace the capital. That is the honest trade.

What I tell other artists

Keep the master. Keep the metadata. Keep one page you control. Use the storefronts as windows, not as the house. If you do that, a platform change is an inconvenience instead of an erasure.
