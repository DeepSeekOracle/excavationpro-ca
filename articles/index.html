What this writing is for

These essays are for listeners who want to know how the catalog works and for independent musicians who are tired of being told that a storefront is an archive. They are written from the practice, not from a marketing brief. The voice is the same one you hear on the records, only slower.

The essays

Why I give the music away explains the difference between a free listen and a free license. How the free listen catalog works covers players, ISRCs, masters, and streams. Notes on experimental hip-hop practice is a session in three rooms.

Independent streaming without a major label is the logistics without the mythology. Live radio, lurk culture, and showing up is about listening rooms that do not demand a performance. Tempo, the practice room, and a free BPM tool is why the browser detector exists.

How to read them

Start with the one whose question you already have. If you are deciding where to publish, read the streaming piece. If you are checking a file, read the catalog piece. If you are making a record, read the practice piece. The links between them are deliberate, but the order is not a course.

What you will not find

No listicles, no affiliate rankings, and no “ten tools that will change your life.” If a page is here, it is because the artist had something to say about the work. If that changes, the page is revised or retired.

What makes a useful music page

A useful music page answers three questions: what is this, how do I hear it, and what am I allowed to do with it. The essays here are built around those questions. They are not reviews, and they are not a substitute for the player. They are the context that makes the player easier to trust.

A note on free tools

The browser BPM detector is free because a tempo check should not require a subscription. It is not a full DAW, and it does not pretend to be one. The writing around the tool explains the limits: a number is a check, not a verdict on the phrase.

Corrections

If a page is wrong, the fix is part of the record. Send the page URL and the sentence through the contact page. The page changes, and the review date changes with it. A quiet deletion is not a correction.

FAQ

Are the essays free?

Yes. They are free to read. The music has its own listen terms; the writing is not behind a paywall.

Can I republish an essay?

Quote a short passage with attribution and a link. Do not republish the full text or sell it. Ask first for anything beyond a quotation.

Where do I start?

Start with the question you already have. The streaming piece is the map; the practice piece is the room; the catalog piece is the receipt.

A note on the catalog

The catalog is the reason these essays exist. A public stream is not a marketing funnel; it is a room with a door. The writing explains the room, the rules, and the work behind it. If you only want the music, the player is one click away. If you want to understand what you are hearing, read on.

What this is not

There is no hidden paid tier, no member-only archive, and no upsell waiting after the last paragraph. If that changes, the page will say so.

It is not a course, a masterclass, or a paid community. There is no upsell at the end of the page. The essays are free because the questions are worth answering in public. The music has its own terms; the writing does not gate the listening.

Why the writing lives here

This site is the artist's own desk. The essays are not syndicated, and they are not written to feed an algorithm. They live next to the music because the music and the explanation belong to the same practice. A reader can move from an idea to a track without leaving the house.
