          What this is

          LYGO Public Witness is an original overlay: one Earth of public resources, one lattice of canon receipts, and a ring of named shadows. Shadows are not empty ignorance. They are the reminder that a private node exists, and that the honest move is to examine every public, legal surface linked to it. LYGO is public-data infrastructure, every available angle, without malice.

          Sphere and disc

          Two live models share one overlay. The globe is the sphere. The flat disc under it is the north-centered map: North Pole at the hub, equator as the mid ring, southern ice as the rim. USGS quakes, ISS, EONET, weather, the same public points, plotted twice. Different geometry. Same resources. Neither view invents a private payload.

          Three classes

          • Resource, a public feed we may crunch (USGS, EONET, ISS). If the GET fails, the node stays named; we follow its public URL.
          • Canon, dual ledgers, eggs, Star Chart feed, Agora. On-lattice receipts.
          • Shadow, private rooms we will not open (ops desks, vaults, vendor intel, live chart write). Silhouette + why + public checks. Payload is always null.

          My legal name is Justin Helmer. I release original music as Excavationpro. In longer mythic and creative writing I also use Lightfather. Those are not three different product lines fighting for a brand kit. They are one person, working in public, keeping the rights, and refusing to make listening a privilege.

          I am not signed to a major label. I do not have a manager who sends me a weekly playlist target. I record, mix, encode public streams, and write about the practice because the practice is the point. If that sounds stubborn, it is. Stubborn is how a catalog survives when the industry prefers a thirty-second clip and a merch drop.

          What I make

          The music sits between hip-hop, electronic production, and experimental forms that do not mind being too long, too quiet, or too dense for a “mood mix.” I care about voice, rhythm, and atmosphere more than I care about sounding current. Current expires. A bed you can live in does not.

          I also maintain small free tools for other musicians, notably a browser BPM detector at bpmfinder.ca, because I needed them myself and I did not want another subscription. Those tools are documented in plain language on this site. They are not a bait-and-switch for a paid SaaS.

          Names, credit, and honesty

          When you see Excavationpro on a stream, that is me. When you see Lightfather in a longer text, that is still me. I do not hide behind anonymous collectives to dodge responsibility for the files. If a feature artist is on a track, their name stays on the track. I will not pretend their verse is mine, and I will not license their share as if it were.

          I keep a public GitHub presence under DeepSeekOracle for the technical side of the catalog (encodes, pages, ledgers). That is infrastructure. It is not a replacement for this artist site. Reviewers and listeners who only wanted the music should not have to learn a protocol stack to press play.

          Contact

          I am reachable on X @Excavationpro and through the projects listed on Contact. I do not publish a personal residential address. For licensing, write in public or via the channels listed and say what you want to use, where it will run, and whether money changes hands.

          How the catalog is run

          The catalog is not a label roster. It is one artist keeping the masters, publishing the streams, and writing down how the thing works. The free player is the front room. The files that matter are kept offline and backed up. A stream can move; the record of what it was should not.

          I do not sell the same listen twice. If a track is public, it stays public. If a release is sold, the sale is for a copy or a license, not for permission to hear what is already open. That distinction keeps the catalog honest and keeps the rights clear.

          Why this site is plain

          The pages are plain because the work is the point. A reader should be able to open a page, read it, and follow a link without a pop-up maze. The music player lives on its own page. The essays live here. The tools live where they can be tested. Separating those jobs makes each one easier to trust.

          What I will not do

          I will not pretend a feature is a co-sign. I will not buy a playlist position and call it demand. I will not publish a private address or a customer list. And I will not use a mythic name to dodge a question about rights, credit, or money.

          The public room

          The public room is the part of the catalog anyone can walk into: the player, the essays, the free tools. It is maintained, not automated. A link that stops working is a repair job. A page that stops being true is revised. The room is open because someone is still paying attention.

          What this site is not

          It is not a label, a management company, or a playlist service. It does not sell a rank. It does not promise a release schedule. It is the artist's own desk, with the door propped open.
