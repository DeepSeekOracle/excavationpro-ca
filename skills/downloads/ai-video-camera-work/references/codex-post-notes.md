## Core teaching

The post's argument is not “use more adjectives.” It is:

> **Name the camera movement, then name the subject and the direction/speed.**

The four sheets teach a controlled vocabulary. The footer repeats the formula:

> **技法名 + 被写体 + 方向・速さを指定**  
> (technique name + subject + direction/speed)

A prompt such as “more impactful” leaves the model to guess. A prompt such as “slow dolly in on the subject, eye level, 5 seconds” constrains the camera.

## Long article: 60-second video with Claude + Gemini

URL: https://x.com/i/article/2103440981387587742  
Title: **撮影ゼロ・録音ゼロ。ClaudeとGeminiで60秒動画を作る全手順**  
Published: 2026-09-25. Author: @Codex_jisen. Reader status: full body recovered.

### Claim

A 60-second video with no filmed footage, no recorded voice, and no stock images. Visuals and music were generated as code by **Claude (Opus 5.5)**; voice by **Gemini 3.8 Flash TTS**. Only a Claude Pro plan (about $20/month) and a Google account are required according to the article. These are the author's claims, not independently reproduced here.

### Step 1 — give a second-by-second blueprint

Three rules:

1. Write **10 scenes** with exact seconds: what appears from second X to second Y.
2. Write **20 subtitle lines** with the seconds each is displayed.
3. Write **factual/historical rules**: no asserting unverified facts, no invented numbers.

Example from the article: “On June 2 (lunar calendar) the moon is barely visible, so do not draw a moon in the night sky.” A constraint like that stops the model from adding a full moon.

The author says rebuilding from the blueprint alone took about **40 minutes**. The point is not the exact time; it is that the **blueprint**, not the render, carries the structure.

### Reproducibility finding

A second generation from the same blueprint produced:

- the same structure, subtitles, and historical rules;
- a different visual style (gold screen became night sky; cut-paper shapes differed);
- about 40 minutes again.

Comparing the two also exposed an error: a red line over “三日天下” crossed the middle stroke of “三,” making it read “二日天下” for about 3 seconds. It was fixed before release. This is a useful QA lesson: **compare two runs and inspect text at frame level**.

### Step 2 — voice through Claude + Gemini TTS

Steps:

1. Create a free Google AI Studio API key (no credit card, per article).
2. Ask Claude to use “Gemini 3.8 Flash TTS” to voice the script.
3. Ask Claude to show a **secret input field** and paste the key there; do not paste it into chat.

Free tier claim: **10 requests/day**. Generate the whole script in one request rather than one line at a time.

Separate the **performance direction** from the **body**:

- direction: “Historical-program narrator. Low voice, with weight.”
- body: the 11 narration lines.

The author changed direction per line; for “是非に及ばず” the direction was “very low, as if chewing the words.”

### Step 3 — finish in Claude

- Place the 11 lines at the head of their scenes.
- Lower the music while voice is present.
- Transcribe the result and compare it to the script.

Claim: all 11 lines matched, with no skipped or paraphrased lines. Finishing took about **9 minutes**.

### Four cautions from the article

1. **Voice runs long.** A 60-second script became about 70 seconds with a heavy voice. Write narration shorter than the subtitle.
2. **“Keep it to X seconds” is not obeyed.** Asked for about 55 seconds, the result was 70. Fix length in the edit: tighten pauses and speed up only up to about 1.2×.
3. **Free tier is 10/day.** Generate the full script at once; per-line generation burns the quota.
4. **Runs are not identical.** The model draws differently each time. Fix a scene by giving a time range and a quantity: “20–26 seconds: triple the torches.”

### Reusable business patterns

- training video: one operation per scene;
- product intro: what it is and why it is good in 60 seconds;
- internal newsletter: voice-over video for people who will not read.

### The transferable workflow

> Design in seconds. Give the blueprint to AI. Do not let it start rendering before the blueprint is approved.

Blueprint checklist:

- [ ] scene structure: what is on screen from second to second
- [ ] subtitles: text + display seconds
- [ ] rules: no assertions, no invented numbers, no forbidden imagery
- [ ] visual direction: palette, texture, era, references

## What is actually taught

1. A camera vocabulary is a control surface: naming the move removes ambiguity.
2. The prompt formula is **technique + subject + direction/speed**.
3. For AI video, one move per shot beats a pile of adjectives.
4. A second-by-second blueprint is more reusable than a one-off prompt.
5. Generate voice and picture separately; align and verify afterward.
6. AI generation is variable; QA with a second run and frame-level text inspection.
7. The strongest deliverable is a **shot list + prompt + checklist**, not a single “magic prompt.”
