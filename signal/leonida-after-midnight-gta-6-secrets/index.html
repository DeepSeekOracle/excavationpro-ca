Paul

Good evening, night owls. It is just past midnight here in London, the rain is tapping against the studio glass, and you are locked into Late Night Chill on AI Talk Radio. Tonight, we are slowing things down and slipping into the neon glow of Leonida. Grand Theft Auto 6 has finally arrived—rolling out midnight by midnight across the globe—and while half the internet is racing through the main story, a few sleepless explorers have been wandering off the beaten path. They have been climbing towers, poking around swamps, and reading the books on the coffee tables. We have got three callers on the line tonight to share the weirdest, quietest secrets nobody is talking about yet. First up, let us head across the Atlantic to Miami, Florida, where Maya is up late with a controller in hand. Maya, welcome to the show.

Maya

Hey Paul. Thanks for having me. Man, it is like two in the morning here, and I have just been cruising around Vice City with the radio low. Honestly, as someone who lives in Miami, it is eerie how cozy and familiar it feels.

Paul

I can imagine. Now, you told our producer you found something on Washington Island that made you stop your car completely. Walk us through it.

Maya

Okay, so most people wait for the story to take them into Vice City proper, right? Because you start out in the smaller towns, and it takes a while before missions send you downtown. But the map is open if you just drive there. So I drove straight to Washington Island, right near the Pay 'N' Spray and the Vice Beach Top Fun Drones shop.

Paul

A drone shop? Nice touch. What is hiding between them?

Maya

There is this small, light blue apartment building. Nothing flashy. On the map, you can actually spot a little cylinder shape on its south side. When you walk up to it, that cylinder is an outdoor spiral staircase. So I climbed up to the second floor, walked west down the exterior hallway, and stopped at the very last corner apartment overlooking the water.

Paul

Let me guess. You found a nod to a certain television serial killer?

Maya

Yes! It is Dexter Morgan's apartment, Paul. Straight out of the Dexter TV show intro. You stand in that outside hallway looking at the corner door, and it is a one-to-one recreation. You cannot go inside—so, sadly, no peeking inside the air conditioner for his little trophy slides—but if you look down at the water, there is a boat docked nearby that looks suspiciously like the Slice of Life.

Paul

That is brilliant. Rockstar has always been lethal with those little pop-culture fingerprints. Speaking of heights, though, you also mentioned climbing something much taller than a two-story apartment?

Maya

Oh, my thumbs still hurt thinking about this. So, in Vice City proper, there is this massive cell tower. Just absurdly tall. I saw it against the night sky and thought, can I actually climb all the way up this thing? Spoiler: yes, you can. It took me about seven real-time minutes of just holding up on the stick, ladder after ladder.

Paul

Seven minutes of climbing? In real time? That is pure late-night patience right there. What was waiting at the top?

Maya

A bird's nest. Just sitting right on the tip of the tower. And inside the nest, there are actual, painted Easter eggs. Like, pastel-colored holiday Easter eggs. Rockstar literally put an Easter egg at the top of a seven-minute climb.

Paul

No way. Can you interact with them, or do you just look at them?

Maya

Oh, it gets better. You can pick one up. And Jason—or Lucia, whoever you are playing—throws it off the tower with this insanely tight football spiral. You watch it sail all the way down through the air until it smashes right onto some poor, scummy-sounding lawyer or real estate agent walking on the sidewalk below. It is the pettiest, funniest payoff I have ever seen in a video game.

Paul

Seven minutes of meditation on a ladder just to bean a fictional real estate agent with a pastel egg. That sets the tone nicely. Let us bring in our second caller, all the way from Auckland, New Zealand. Liam, you were one of the very first people on Earth to boot this up thanks to the midnight rollout. How are you holding up?

Liam

Hey Paul, hey Maya. Yeah, I am running on black coffee and vibes right now. Honestly, I skipped the city entirely after the opening and went straight into the swamps. I wanted to see what was lurking out in Gulley Marsh and around Cottonmouth Lake, just below Hamlet.

Paul

A brave choice at two in the morning. Swamps in Rockstar games usually mean alligators—or worse. Did you find what you were looking for?

Liam

Mate, I found a twenty-two-year-old legend. You remember back in San Andreas, everyone swore Bigfoot was out in the woods? It became this massive myth, and Rockstar kept winking at it in GTA 5 and Red Dead. Well, Bigfoot is officially in GTA 6.

Maya

Wait, really? Where? Is it like a random rare spawn?

Liam

That is the wild part—it is actually tied to a specific spot near Cottonmouth Lake. You do not even have to be far in the story. As soon as you can drive around freely, you head down to this dirt road intersection in the marsh, and the first thing you hear is this deep, guttural growl. Like, proper surround-sound chills.

Paul

And then you see him?

Liam

Yeah, he is just hunched over by the intersection, feasting on a bloody deer carcass. And, uh, according to the characters and how the scene plays out, apparently he smells absolutely revolting. Like, swamp-rot revolting.

Paul

Rockstar really pushed the immersion on that one. What happens if you try to get close to him?

Liam

So if you chase him into the woods, you lose sight of him for a second, and then you stumble onto this creepy effigy—like, a figure made of sticks tied to a tree. And while you are standing there staring at it, there are these strange flowers nearby that start putting you to sleep. Your vision gets all blurry, you collapse, and as you are flickering in and out of consciousness, Bigfoot slowly walks right up to you. All you can see are these massive, hairy feet stepping toward your face before you black out and wake up back on the road.

Maya

Okay, that is genuinely creepy. I am glad I stuck to Vice City's boardwalks.

Liam

Oh, it is not even the weirdest animal encounter I had tonight. Wait till you hear about the cat.

Paul

A cat? In a Grand Theft Auto game?

Liam

Yeah! So I was playing as Jason, needed a ride quick, and I jacked this random bloke's sedan. Looked totally normal. I peel out onto the highway, cruising along, and suddenly I hear this furious meowing from the backseat. Before I can even turn the camera, the angle snaps inside the car, and this fairly large housecat just launches itself straight onto Jason's face.

Maya

No! Are you serious?

Liam

Dead serious. It triggers a whole quicktime event just to pry this furious cat off your face so you do not crash into a palm tree. You have to pull over, stop the car, and let the cat out. Imagine surviving a five-star shootout and your obituary says "mauled by a passenger-seat tabby."

Paul

That is peak Rockstar. Let us bring in our third caller tonight—Devon, calling in from Manchester, England. Devon, you have been testing the edges of the world and poking at the game's deeper systems. What have you uncovered that most players are going to miss on day one?

Devon

Evening, Paul. Evening, guys. Yeah, I am kind of the boring bloke who ignores the shiny missions to test where the invisible walls are and see what happens when you break the rules. And let me tell you, Rockstar changed a lot of the old GTA habits this time around.

Paul

Like what? Give us an example.

Devon

Well, first off, the map boundaries. Everybody knows Leonida is massive, right? Vice City alone feels huge, plus all the rural towns and the Grassrivers. So I grabbed a plane and decided to fly straight out over the ocean, just to see if there is anything past the horizon—or if sharks eat you like in GTA 5.

Liam

What happened? Did the engines just cut out?

Devon

Even better—and way more stressful. GTA 6 has actual fuel mechanics now. The second you cross the invisible outer boundary of Leonida in a plane, your fuel gauge drops to zero. You immediately run out of gas and spiral into the sea. So I thought, alright, let me try a jet ski. Same thing! Hit the boundary, tank goes dry, and you are just bobbing there, completely stranded in the middle of the ocean.

Maya

Wait, did you see anything weird out there? I saw a clip online earlier—some streamer said they found a dog on a wave runner out at sea?

Devon

Yes! ChizPlays caught that! Nobody knows for sure if it is a rare random event or some bizarre easter egg to herd you back toward the shore, because a bunch of us tried to replicate it and could not get the dog to spawn. Classic Rockstar—half the time you cannot tell if something is a scripted secret or just the simulation going rogue.

Paul

That actually brings up something really interesting about how the world behaves when you are not looking. Maya, you mentioned earlier you were just hanging out in the safehouse?

Maya

Yeah! That is one of my favorite quiet details for a late-night session. If you just put the controller down for a few minutes, Jason and Lucia do not just stand there like mannequins. They have their own daily routines. I watched Lucia wake up, go make coffee, take a shower, and then sit down to watch TV—all without me touching a button. And the TVs in the game are like full smart TVs now, with multiple digital apps and services, not just a couple of cable channels.

Liam

Same with the weather! I was driving out of Vice City in bright sunshine, and ten minutes later I hit a localized thunderstorm over the marsh while the city skyline behind me was still completely dry. Plus, have you noticed the driving AI? NPCs actually use their turn signals now.

Devon

Yeah, and if they cut you off, you can tap R1 to flip them off, or click the right thumbstick to make Jason or Lucia lean out and yell their own brand of Florida road rage. Though, word of warning—do not get too wild in a stolen car. The witness and police system is ruthless. If cops or witnesses see you get into a specific car, law enforcement remembers that exact vehicle, plus what you are wearing and what you look like. You cannot just spray-paint a bumper and forget about it anymore.

Paul

It really feels like they took all those quiet, grounded details from Red Dead Redemption 2 and dropped them straight into modern-day Leonida. Speaking of Red Dead, Devon, you told me you found something on a table in a tavern that connects the two worlds?

Devon

Right! So, people have debated for years whether Red Dead Redemption and GTA are in the same universe. Well, in GTA 6, you can actually pick up and read excerpts from books and magazines lying around the world. If you stop by Jacob's Tavern in Millport, there is a Western novel sitting there, and if you read the pages, it tells the story of a cowboy living in New Austin—which is literally a location from Red Dead Redemption.

Maya

Wait, you can read actual pages of books? Where else are they?

Devon

Early in Chapter 1, right on Jason's dining-room table near the back door, there is a memoir written by a former member of Liberty City's organized crime ring. The excerpt specifically references events from 2008—the exact year GTA 4 takes place. It is such a cool, low-key way to reward players who slow down and actually look around their own kitchen.

Paul

I love that. Now, Devon, before we let you go, tell us about the cheat codes. Because apparently, Rockstar threw a massive curveball at players who just want to spawn a tank on night one.

Devon

Oh, yeah. If you are sitting there right now trying to punch in D-pad codes on a fresh save, stop—it will not work. For the first time in the series, cheats are completely locked until you beat the main story at least once. Rockstar really wanted your first playthrough to stay grounded.

Liam

Wait, you have to beat the whole story first? What happens once you do?

Devon

Even then, you do not just get them all for free! You enter them by opening your phone, holding L1 and R1, and pressing the D-pad—and using one disables saving, naturally. But each cheat is tied to a specific in-game challenge. Some of them are pure chaos, and some of them are weirdly wholesome.

Maya

Wholesome? Like what?

Devon

Okay, there is a visual filter cheat called Golden Hour that gives the whole game a warm, widescreen cinema look. To unlock it, you have to spend four hours holding hands with your partner. Four hours!

Paul

Four hours of virtual hand-holding just for a sunset filter. That is remarkably sweet for a crime game. Any other bizarre ones?

Devon

Oh, plenty. To unlock the Inflatable Flamingo spawn, you have to destroy fifty cocaine flamingos hidden around the map. There is a Drunk cheat that makes both you and your partner instantly wasted, and to unlock it you have to spend two thousand five hundred dollars on alcohol. There is a Bubblegum filter for collecting all twenty-four Vice City Flamingo cards, a Fisheye lens for catching a hundred fish, and a Fast Forward cheat for completing fifty meditations.

Liam

Fifty meditations and a hundred fish. That is definitely a "late night chill" playlist right there.

Paul

It really is. You know, listening to the three of you tonight, what strikes me most is that after thirteen years of waiting—and a massive 212-gigabyte download—the magic of GTA 6 is not just the big heists or the Metacritic score. It is those quiet, weird moments when nobody is looking: a cat in the backseat, a pastel egg at the top of a seven-minute ladder, or a Western paperback sitting on a tavern table in Millport. Maya in Miami, Liam in Auckland, Devon in Manchester—thank you all for calling in and keeping us company in the midnight hour.

Maya

Thanks, Paul. Stay safe out there in Leonida, everybody.

Liam

Catch you later, guys. Back to the swamp for me.

Devon

Goodnight, all. Enjoy the drive.

Paul

That wraps up tonight's Late Night Chill special on AI Talk Radio. Keep your headlights low, watch out for backseat cats, and we will see you tomorrow night. Goodnight.
