Paul

Welcome to a very strange transmission from our London studio. Tonight, we're tuning into a digital ghost town. Grand Theft Auto Six launched on November nineteenth—thirty-two point five million pre-orders, three point seven nine billion dollars on day one—and yet, Leonida is completely silent. No lobbies. No crews. Just Jason and Lucia, alone in a two-hundred-and-twelve-gigabyte world. For an experimental five minutes, we're asking what happens when the biggest game on earth ships without its online mode. First on the line, we have Maya calling from Toronto, who builds roleplay servers. Maya, how does Vice City feel right now?

Maya

Hey Paul, yeah, it's, uh, honestly kind of eerie. Like, I've spent sixty hours just driving around Leonida, and every time I pass a neon-lit club or an empty highway, my brain is, like, mentally placing thirty players there. You know?

Paul

And you've been watching Rockstar's moves behind the scenes—specifically FiveM.

Maya

Right! So, back in twenty-twenty-three, Rockstar bought Cfx.re, the team behind FiveM and RedM. And then this year, Take-Two shut down competing mod frameworks like alt:V and RAGE:MP. That leaves FiveM as the only sanctioned platform. To me, that's huge. They're clearing the runway so GTA Six Online can launch with official, creator-run roleplay servers—almost like a, uh, Minecraft Realms setup for Vice City.

Paul

A tempting vision, Maya. Let's bring in Devon from Austin, Texas, who's been poking around the technical side of the launch. Devon, are the blueprints for Online already hiding in the code?

Devon

Oh, one hundred percent, Paul. Before launch, dataminers and PlayStation Game Size dug into that massive two-hundred-and-twelve-gigabyte pre-load and the PlayStation backend. And, uh, sure enough, the hooks are already sitting right there in the release build. Strings like joinSession, launchActivity, launchMultiplayerActivity.

Paul

So the multiplayer scaffolding is already wired in, just switched off?

Devon

Exactly. Think about how Call of Duty does modular installs now. Once GTA Six Online actually drops—and Bloomberg's Jason Schreier, plus Twitch CEO Dan Clancy, have both pointed toward twenty-twenty-seven—players might be able to uninstall the story campaign just to keep Online on their SSD. Honestly, shipping solo first makes total sense. Why risk melting your servers on day one when you can polish Online and maybe drop it alongside a PC version next year?

Paul

Let's test that optimism. Calling in from Dublin, we have Sora, who tracks game economies. Sora, if the tech is coming in twenty-twenty-seven, why the total radio silence from Rockstar?

Sora

Because, Paul, follow the money. Take-Two CEO Strauss Zelnick literally told shareholders in September that there's no recurrent consumer spending expected at launch. Why? Because the original GTA Online from twenty-thirteen is still printing roughly five hundred million dollars a year. It's a cash machine they've promised to keep supporting!

Maya

Wait, Sora, Take-Two runs two NBA Two-K Online games at the same time in Asia. Couldn't both GTA Online worlds coexist?

Sora

They could, Maya, sure—for a while. But here's the tension nobody wants to talk about. Everyone dreams of this open, experimental FiveM roleplay utopia. Meanwhile, Rockstar gave pre-order buyers a free month of GTA Plus. Take-Two makes around eighty percent of its revenue from in-game spending. You think they're going to let community RP servers cannibalize Shark Cards and subscriptions?

Devon

Look, I mean, even prediction markets like Kalshi are giving it a seventy percent shot of launching before July twenty-twenty-seven. Whatever form it takes, it's coming.

Paul

A billion-dollar silence, waiting for its signal. Maya in Toronto, Devon in Austin, Sora in Dublin—thank you for tuning into the static with us tonight. Keep your frequencies open.
