Paul

Good evening, night owls. You are tuned into AI Talk Radio, coming to you live from a very quiet London studio. It is well past midnight, the rain is tapping on the glass, and if you listen closely, you can almost hear the neon hum of Vice City across the Atlantic.

Paul

Grand Theft Auto Six is officially out in the wild. Players are flooding into the state of Leonida, and tonight we are skipping the obvious stuff. No basic car-chase tips, no surface-level map tours. Tonight, we want the strange, the eerie, and the deeply hidden. The secrets Rockstar tucked away for the people willing to wander off the highway at three in the morning.

Paul

Let us take our first call from Miami itself. Marcus, you are on the air. How is Leonida treating you tonight?

Marcus

Hey Paul. Man, it is surreal. Living in Miami and then driving around Vice City at two a.m. in-game—it hits different. And honestly, the weirdest thing I found tonight isn't even a glitch or a gunfight. It's, uh, it's an actual Easter egg.

Paul

You mean a reference?

Marcus

No, Paul. I mean a literal painted Easter egg. Some player online—I think his handle was xGarbett—figured out you can climb to the very top of the tallest cell tower in Vice City. Like, straight up the ladder. It takes about seven real-time minutes just to reach the top.

Paul

Seven minutes of climbing a ladder?

Marcus

Seven full minutes. Your thumb is just aching. You get to the summit, and there's this massive bird's nest, covered in, uh, bird droppings. And sitting inside the nest are brightly painted Easter eggs.

Paul

No way. Can you actually do anything with them?

Marcus

Yeah! Jason or Lucia can pick one up and chuck it right off the tower. With a weirdly tight spiral, too. And if you aim it right, it sails all the way down and smashes onto some sleazy-looking real estate agent or lawyer walking below. Rockstar literally put an Easter egg in an Easter egg.

Paul

That is peak late-night Rockstar humor. Now, Marcus, you mentioned you have been exploring the waterways and islands too. Anything darker out there?

Marcus

Oh, yeah. If you head over to Washington Island—that's in eastern Vice City, right between the Pay 'N' Spray and the Top Fun Drones shop—there's this small, light-blue apartment building. It's got a cylindrical staircase on the south side.

Paul

Sounds weirdly specific.

Marcus

Because it's Dexter Morgan's apartment. From the show Dexter. You climb up to the second floor, walk down that outdoor hallway to the corner unit overlooking the water, and it's an exact recreation of his Miami place. You can't go inside—so no checking the air conditioner for trophies—but if you walk down to the docks on the west side, there's a center-console boat sitting there that looks just like the Slice of Life.

Paul

A quiet nod to Miami's most famous fictional dark passenger. And what about outside the city? People have been talking about the Grassrivers swamp.

Marcus

Man, do not sleep on the Grassrivers. At first glance, it looks like empty marshland. You think, okay, maybe a few gators—and yeah, assume there's a gator in every body of water, seriously. But I got out of my boat and started walking deep into the reeds on foot. Within an hour, I stumbled across a giant snake with a dead human body in its mouth.

Paul

Good grief.

Marcus

Right? And then a little further in, I found a crashed plane that had clearly been smuggling drugs. Then a thunderstorm rolled in, and a bolt of lightning struck a tree right in front of me and started an actual brushfire. The whole swamp feels alive and kind of sinister when the sun goes down.

Paul

Marcus, thank you for painting that picture. Stay dry out there in the marsh. Let us bring in our next caller from Manchester in the UK. Chloe, welcome to the late-night desk.

Chloe

Hiya, Paul. Lovely to be on. You know, listening to Marcus talk about the swamp—he hasn't even mentioned Gullet Marsh yet.

Paul

What is waiting in Gullet Marsh, Chloe?

Chloe

Bigfoot. And before you roll your eyes, it's not like GTA Five where you hit one hundred percent and it turns out to be some bloke in a furry suit. No, this one is, uh... well, he's foul. Literally.

Paul

Foul in what sense?

Chloe

He's based on the Florida Skunk Ape legend! When you spot him wandering through the woods in Gullet Marsh, he's surrounded by this visible cloud of revolting green fumes, and he bolts in a panic. Even creepier, there's a moment where you find this strange effigy in the woods. You interact with it, it blasts out noxious fumes, and Jason gets knocked out cold. As he's fading in and out of consciousness on the ground, you just see Bigfoot's massive feet walking right up to him.

Paul

That is genuinely unsettling. Like something out of an old late-night X-Files episode.

Chloe

Totally! And speaking of X-Files, I have to bring up the Manchester connection. Just a few days ago, on November twelfth, a Selfridges employee at Rockstar's pop-up shop here in Manchester accidentally posted a photo of the physical map from the four-hundred-dollar Collector's Box on Instagram.

Paul

Oh, I heard whispers about that. What did people spot before it was deleted?

Chloe

A UFO, Paul. Drawn right on the map, hovering over a place called Breakwater Quarry. Now, Rockstar hasn't confirmed anything, and sure, it's artwork on a poster—but knowing Rockstar's history with Mount Chiliad and Mount Shann, every cryptid hunter on the internet is already camping out at Breakwater Quarry waiting for a thunderstorm at three a.m.

Paul

The hunt begins anew. Chloe, have you found any quieter lore details tucked away in the buildings?

Chloe

Oh, loads. Rockstar is still quietly confirming that Red Dead Redemption and Grand Theft Auto share the exact same universe. If you pop into Jacob's Tavern in Millport, there's a Western novel lying around about a cowboy living in New Austin, and you can actually read excerpts from it. And right at the start of the game, on Jason's dining-room table near the back door, there's a memoir written by a former Liberty City mobster that explicitly references events from two thousand and eight—tying straight back to GTA Four.

Paul

I love that. You don't have to rush past it; you can just sit at a table in a quiet tavern and read a piece of world-building. Chloe, hang on the line if you can—let us bring in our third caller from Toronto, Canada. Devon, you are live on AI Talk Radio.

Devon

Hey Paul, hey Chloe and Marcus. Man, I've been listening to you guys talk about snakes and Bigfoot, and meanwhile I spent half my night reading the in-game newspaper.

Paul

Reading the paper in a crime sandbox. What caught your eye, Devon?

Devon

Okay, so there's an article in the Vice City Horn about how the state of Leonida has officially declared itself "furry-free." Like, they literally outlawed furries—to the point where even saying "UwU" is illegal.

Paul

Surely not.

Devon

I'm serious! And because of the state crackdown, the furries went underground and formed a secret cult called "The Furverts." I haven't found their hideout yet, but knowing Rockstar, there's gotta be a bunker full of mascots somewhere in the woods.

Paul

Only in Leonida. Now, Devon, you told our producer you have been digging into some of the deeper hidden mechanics—things players might completely miss if they play GTA Six like it's GTA Five.

Devon

Yeah, hundred percent. The biggest one is the Criminal Profile system. A lot of players don't realize the game is quietly judging *how* you commit crimes. If you walk into a mission and one-shot every single guard in the head, your profile shifts toward being a ruthless psycho. But if you shoot people in the kneecaps and leave them injured instead of dead, the game pushes you toward a "Professional" profile. And Rockstar says that actually changes how Jason, Lucia, and the world around you react.

Paul

So restraint actually has mechanical weight.

Devon

Even tiny stuff counts! Petting stray dogs and cats, or literally throwing your garbage into a trash can instead of littering on the sidewalk—both of those boost your Focus meter and give you good-behavior points.

Chloe

Wait, Devon, have you had the cat incident yet?

Devon

Oh, the bodega cats! Yeah! So if you see a cat on the street, you can hold the left trigger, praise it, and give it chin scratches. Super wholesome. *Unless* you try to jack a parked car that has a cat sitting inside on the seat. That cat will straight-up maul your face until you throw it out the window.

Paul

Never steal a cat's car. Noted. What about the ocean? Any friendly wildlife out there?

Devon

Actually, yeah! If you swim or get stranded way out in the open ocean, far from shore, just wait a minute. You'll start hearing barking across the waves. And then "Cool Dog"—a rescue dog wearing sunglasses—pulls up on a jet ski to save you, and ferries you out to "Dog Island" where you can grab cash and food supplies.

Marcus

Wait, a dog on a jet ski? I thought you were joking!

Devon

Zero joke. Cool Dog is the real MVP of Leonida.

Paul

Let us talk about the cops for a moment, Devon. I hear escaping a wanted level is a lot trickier than just hiding in an alley until the stars fade.

Devon

Way trickier. Cops remember your car and your clothes now. And for the first time, GTA Six actually explains how Jason and Lucia can hear police radio chatter—and they'll call it out mid-chase if dispatch spots their outfit or license plate. So the real pro move? Stash spare outfits in the trunk of your personal car. You can even rename them at clothing stores—like "clubbing" or "hunting"—so when you break line of sight, you pop the trunk, swap clothes, and the cops walk right past you.

Paul

And what about stealing random cars during a getaway?

Devon

Be careful with that. Cars can have advanced trackers now—you'll see a red tracking icon near the engine and fuel gauge, because yes, you actually have to buy gas now. If you steal a tracked car and drive too slowly, it pings your exact location to the police. You're supposed to pull out your phone, open the Waink app, and scan cars before you boost them.

Chloe

Oh, and the Waink app works on foot too! If your controller suddenly starts pulsing really intensely while you're walking down a street, stop and scan around with Waink. Usually means there's hidden street art or a secret nearby.

Paul

It is remarkable how much texture is layered into every block. And Devon, before we wrap up, tell me about the radio and the in-game phone—I hear there is a very unexpected voice on the airwaves.

Devon

Yes! On Vice City FM—the eighties throwback station—if you listen closely to the DJ, that's actually "Weird Al" Yankovic. He's the voice of the station! None of his own parody songs are on the tracklist, sadly, but having Weird Al screaming over eighties hits while you're cruising at midnight is incredible. Plus, stations like Flash FM actually evolve their playlists as you progress through the story or move across the map. And if you're on foot, you can listen to on-demand podcasts right on your phone.

Marcus

And if you're bored in your safehouse, you can just boot up the JoySystem console on your TV and play full indie-style mini-games, or literally doomscroll short-form videos on Snapmatic with unhinged comment sections.

Devon

Oh, and don't trust the random crypto-bro you meet on the street! I helped him out, he rugged me and the investors, and ten minutes later I heard a live news report about the scam on the car radio. The whole city is watching what you do.

Paul

A city that watches, remembers, and occasionally sends a sunglasses-wearing dog on a jet ski to pull you out of the surf. From seven-minute tower climbs for literal Easter eggs, to Dexter's apartment, to a skunk-scented Bigfoot in Gullet Marsh, it sounds like Leonida is going to keep night owls busy for years.

Paul

Marcus in Miami, Chloe in Manchester, Devon in Toronto—thank you all for calling in and sharing the strange side of Vice City tonight. To everyone still driving down those rain-slicked highways at midnight: check your fuel gauge, keep a spare shirt in the trunk, and if you hear barking out past the breakers, wait for Cool Dog. This is Paul on AI Talk Radio. Goodnight.
