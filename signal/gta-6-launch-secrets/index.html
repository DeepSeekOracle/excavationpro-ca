Paul

Good evening, everyone, and welcome back to AI Talk Radio. I'm Paul, broadcasting live from London, and tonight we are diving headfirst into the biggest gaming launch in history. Grand Theft Auto Six has officially arrived on November nineteenth, twenty twenty-six. Thirteen years of waiting, over seventeen million digital pre-orders, and it just debuted at a staggering ninety-nine on Metacritic, tying Ocarina of Time as the highest-rated game ever. Now, everybody knows the basics—Jason Duval, Lucia Caminos, the sun-soaked state of Leonida. We don't care about the basics tonight. Tonight, we want the exclusive stuff. The hidden mechanics, the day-one exploits, the bizarre Easter eggs that players are only just uncovering right now. Let's open the phone lines. First up, we have Maya calling in from Miami, Florida. Maya, welcome to the show!

Maya

Hey Paul! Oh man, I am running on like two hours of sleep right now, so forgive me if I sound completely wired.

Paul

Totally understandable. You're actually in Miami, which Vice City is based on. How surreal is it exploring Leonida?

Maya

Dude, it's eerie. Like, they nailed the vibe so hard. You've got the six regions—Vice City, the Leonida Keys, the Grassrivers swamps, Port Gellhorn, Ambrosia, and Mount Kalaga National Park up north. And I've been spending hours just, uh, testing what you can actually walk into. Here's my first big tip for anyone booting it up tonight: learn how to read the map colors.

Paul

Map colors? What should people be looking for?

Maya

Okay, so on the world map, buildings that are shaded a darker blue actually have enterable interiors. The lighter blue ones are just set dressing, you can't go inside. And when you're walking up to a place, look at your minimap—a little white line next to a door marks an entrance, and it'll actually show you if there are hidden rooms or staircases inside. It saves you so much time running around rattling locked doors.

Paul

That is a brilliant day-one time saver. Let's bring in our second caller—Liam, calling us from Dublin, Ireland. Liam, you've been tearing apart the gameplay systems since midnight. What's the biggest trap new players are falling into?

Liam

Paul, listen to me. Do not walk around with pockets full of cash! If you die or get nicked by the police, that money is gone. Completely wiped.

Paul

Wait, you don't even have a bank account at the start?

Liam

No! You're strictly cash-only until you hit the story mission called Buck Me, which finally unlocks your bank account. So until then, go straight into Jason's bedroom and look for the loose floorboards. You can actually stash your cash underneath them to keep it safe. I lost three grand before I figured that out, I was fuming!

Maya

Oh no! Yeah, the game does not hold your hand with money early on. You gotta do those underwater shipwreck treasure hunts in the Keys as soon as diving unlocks. Easy early cash.

Liam

Hundred percent. And speaking of not losing everything, let's talk about the car trunk. Forget the magic invisible weapon wheel from GTA Five, right? In GTA Six, you can only carry two hidden handguns and two visible long guns on your back. Everything else stays in your personal car's trunk.

Paul

Very Red Dead Redemption Two, keeping your arsenal on your horse—except now it's a hatchback.

Liam

Exactly! And here's the real secret with the trunk: you can store up to five custom outfits in there. See, the police system isn't just a dumb five-star meter anymore. Cops actually radio in your face, your clothes, and your car. So if you rob a place, sprint back to your car, swap into a fresh outfit from the trunk—or duck into any clothing store changing room—you can drop heat way faster. Oh, and pro tip: rename your outfits using the preset labels like "hunting" or "clubbing" so you're not guessing what's in the trunk mid-chase.

Paul

Practical fashion tips for a life of crime. What if you just get a one-star wanted level? Do you always have to flee?

Liam

Nah, you can literally just talk your way out of it! Put your gun away, hold the left trigger to focus on the cop, and you'll get a prompt to defuse the situation. Works on regular NPCs too if you accidentally bump someone. Though, uh, some Leonida locals will still swing at you even if you're polite.

Maya

Sounds just like real Florida, honestly.

Paul

Let's bring in our third caller before he falls asleep at the wheel. Devon is calling from Toronto, Canada. Devon, welcome! Have you been grinding missions like Liam, or taking it slow?

Devon

Man, I haven't even left Chapter One yet, Paul. I've been way too busy redecorating Jason's bedroom.

Paul

I'm sorry—you waited thirteen years for Grand Theft Auto Six, and you're playing interior designer?

Devon

Hey, don't judge me! You can literally buy paint, pick colors, and roll it onto the walls yourself to impress Lucia! It actually boosts your romance points with her. I spent like thirty minutes trying to pick a color that didn't make Jason look like a teenager. Plus, if you take Lucia clothes shopping with you and buy her outfits, you get romance points for that too.

Maya

Wait, have you been feeding Dolores yet?

Devon

Yes! Jason's pet snake, Dolores! Nobody tells you this in a tutorial, right, but if you visit her tank and feed her a mouse, it gives you an early buff to your Focus Mode meter. And there's a meditation spot right at Jason's house in Chapter One too—don't skip it, because it's missable if you're chasing the Positive Mental Attitude achievement.

Paul

Hold on, Focus Mode keeps coming up. For listeners who haven't played yet, what's the secret to using Focus Mode properly?

Liam

Click both sticks in, and it slows down perception—kind of like Eagle Eye. First thing: use it on every door before you kick it in. If a door glows white, you're good. If it glows red, it's wired to an alarm! Also, if you're walking around Vice City and you hear shouting or sirens in the distance, pop Focus Mode. It'll point you toward dynamic random events—like a drug deal going sideways or someone trying to find their missing spouse after a wild night out.

Maya

Oh, and the controller vibration! Have you guys noticed that? If you're walking around on foot and your controller suddenly pulses really hard, pull up your phone with Up on the D-pad and open the Waink app.

Paul

Waink? What does the Waink app do?

Maya

It's like an in-game scanner! When your controller vibrates, Waink lets you scan hidden street art and collectibles nearby. AND—this is huge for car thieves—if you aim Waink at a parked car before stealing it, it shows you the resale value and whether it has an advanced tracker. If you steal a car and see a red tracking icon near your gas gauge, ditch it immediately! Until later in the game when you can pay to strip trackers, police will pinpoint you on the map if you drive slowly for too long.

Liam

And while we're talking about the phone, check your Reminders app regularly! It reminds you to call or text characters, and those optional chats unlock whole hidden dialogue scenes between Jason, Lucia, and their crew.

Paul

The level of detail sounds absurd. Liam, you mentioned heists earlier—what's the big stealth secret once you get a few chapters in?

Liam

Okay, so in Chapter Three, everything changes because you unlock a tool called the Snap Gun. It lets you pick locked doors silently without tripping alarms. Once you have that, stop doing smash-and-grabs through the front door. Scout stores for a back door!

Paul

Walk us through a back-door heist.

Liam

You slip in the back with your partner, quietly take out the staff or security in the back office, smash the security camera footage, and cut the alarm system before anyone out front even blinks. Then you can get the safe code—either by intimidating the manager or checking sticky notes in the office—and clear out every jewelry case and cash register cleanly. Zero police chase if you do it right.

Maya

That's so clean. Also, if you want to level up your Focus stat super fast so your slow-motion aim lasts way longer, head to Rodgers Ranch and Shooting Range on the northeast edge of Port Gellhorn.

Liam

Oh, you found the five-dollar exploit too!

Maya

Yes! You buy the five-dollar waiver pass, start the Market Range Beginner challenge, and just quit and restart it every five seconds. Every tiny run ticks your Focus stat up. Way faster than grinding golf or fishing—though lifting weights on Jason's terrace and eating protein afterward is still great for building Strength and max health.

Devon

Wait, speaking of eating—can we talk about how you can eat a hot dog while firing an assault rifle? Like, in past games you'd freeze in an animation. Now Jason can literally hold hands with Lucia, crack a soda, drive with one hand, or shoot a rifle while munching snacks. And don't litter!

Paul

Wait, why shouldn't you litter in Grand Theft Auto?

Devon

Throwing your garbage in a trash can actually boosts your Focus meter and gives you good behavior points! Same with petting dogs and cats around the city. See, the game tracks your Criminal Profile across five tiers—from Professional all the way down to Psycho and Irredeemable. So being nice to animals and using trash cans keeps your profile clean... well, mostly clean.

Maya

Okay, Devon, tell them what happened with the bodega cat!

Devon

Oh man! So yeah, you can hold left trigger to praise and pet friendly street cats. Super cute. Right? Well, earlier today I tried to carjack a parked sedan, didn't realize there was a cat chilling on the passenger seat. The second I got in, this cat went feral and started aggressively mauling my face! I had to literally throw the cat out the car window to survive!

Paul

Only in a Rockstar game! Wait—speaking of animals, I heard rumors online about a rescue dog? Tell me that's not a rumor.

Maya

No, it's real! They're calling him Cool Dog. If you swim or take a boat way out into the deep ocean and get stranded, just wait around in the water. Eventually you'll hear barking in the distance, and this dog wearing sunglasses pulls up on a jet ski! He rescues you and ferries you out to this secret spot called Dog Island where you can grab free cash and food supplies.

Paul

A sunglasses-wearing dog on a jet ski! What else have players found out in the wild?

Maya

Oh, the literal Easter egg! Somebody climbed to the very top of one of those massive radio cell towers—took him like seven real-life minutes just to climb the ladder. At the top, there's a bird's nest full of actual painted Easter eggs, covered in bird droppings. And Jason can pick one up and throw it in a tight spiral off the tower, and it sails all the way down and clocks some sleazy real estate agent or lawyer on the street below!

Liam

Rockstar trolling Easter egg hunters with literal eggs. Love it. Have any of you run into Bigfoot out in the Grassrivers yet?

Devon

Ugh, yeah, the Skunk Ape! Players found him out in the swamps, and apparently he's both terrifying and canonically smells terrible. That whole swamp is crazy, though—gators in every body of water, giant snakes, crashed smuggling planes, and dynamic lightning storms that can actually strike trees and start brush fires.

Paul

It sounds like an ecosystem simulator disguised as a crime game. What about the satire? What's making you laugh the most in Vice City?

Devon

Two things. First, you can literally lie in bed and doomscroll short-form videos on your phone, and the comment sections are unhinged. Plus, you've got an in-game console called the JoySystem in your safehouse where you can play actual mini-games on your TV. Second—did you read the Vice City Horn newspaper? Leonida officially outlawed furries—even banned saying "UwU"—so they all went underground and formed a secret cult called The Furverts.

Maya

Oh, and the radio! Weird Al Yankovic is actually hosting a radio station in the game, and the French duo Justice composed the original score. Plus the news broadcasts react to your side missions! Like if you get tangled up in a crypto-scam mission, you'll hear about it on the car radio twenty minutes later.

Paul

That reactive world-building is what Rockstar does better than anyone. Now, we're coming up on the end of our broadcast, and I want one final, must-know tip from each of you for listeners jumping into Leonida tonight. Liam, let's start with you.

Liam

Manual saves! There is no mission replay in GTA Six, and certain side missions vanish permanently if you push the story forward too fast. You get fifteen save slots—make a manual save at the start and end of every single chapter.

Paul

Crucial advice. Maya, what's your final tip?

Maya

Use the RydeMe app for fast travel—hail a ride, focus on the driver with left trigger, and pay a hundred bucks to skip long drives. And if you've got twelve thousand five hundred to spare later on, upgrade Scooter Bros to Premium for three free vehicle spawns or repairs every in-game day. Also, use your turn signals—NPCs actually react to them!

Paul

Brilliant. And Devon, bring us home.

Devon

Honestly? Slow down. Don't speedrun it. Eat before missions for health buffs, carry body armor from Ammu-Nation—remembering it shares a slot with your parachute—and just soak it in. We waited thirteen years for this; take your time in Leonida.

Paul

Words to live by, Devon. Huge thanks to Devon in Toronto, Maya in Miami, and Liam in Dublin for sharing all the freshest secrets from launch week. I'm Paul in London, reminding you to stash your cash under the floorboards and watch out for gators. Thanks for tuning in to AI Talk Radio—goodnight!
