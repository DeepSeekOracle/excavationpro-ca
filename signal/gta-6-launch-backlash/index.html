Paul

Good evening, and welcome back to the show. I'm Paul, broadcasting live from our London studio, and tonight there is really only one topic dominating every timeline on the planet. Grand Theft Auto Six has finally arrived. Thirteen years after GTA Five, two delays, nineteen million pre-orders, and a staggering ninety-nine out of a hundred on Metacritic, tying Ocarina of Time.

Paul

You'd think everyone would be celebrating, right? Well, open up X or Reddit today and it looks like a completely different universe. Clips of trucks flying across the map, players furious they can't easily steal cars, complaints about thirty frames per second, and people calling it The Sims with guns. Tonight we're having a proper, unvarnished conversation about GTA Six's game design, its performance, and whether thirteen years of hype just collided with reality. Let's go straight to the phones. First up, we've got Devon calling in from Miami—fittingly enough, the real-life Vice City. Devon, you're on the air.

Devon

Hey, Paul. Yeah, man, I took the day off work, stayed up for the midnight unlock on my base PS5, and honestly? I'm kind of stunned. And not in a good way.

Paul

Stunned how? You've been waiting over a decade for this. What happened when you actually got your hands on the controller?

Devon

Look, the game is called Grand Theft Auto, right? Like, it's literally in the title. So I walk up to a nice car on the street, hit the button to jack it, and... I can't even hotwire it because it's a modern keyless car. Then I try another one with a driver inside, and instead of just tossing him out and peeling away, I'm stuck in a button-mashing QTE because the NPC is fighting me back! And if the door's locked, you can't even slim-jim it with someone inside, you gotta smash the window and alert half the block.

Paul

I did see a thread on X earlier—someone said by making it so realistic, Rockstar sacrificed the zero-friction chaos that made GTA fun in the first place. Someone else joked they were going to dust off their old Saints Row Two disc.

Devon

Exactly! It feels like, uh, like chores disguised as a crime game. I saw this post on X comparing it to The Sims with guns, and I laughed at first, and then three hours in I realized I was literally walking around a shopping mall, buying clothes, ordering food at the food court, and checking my couple rating with Lucia. Like, where's the arcade mayhem?

Paul

Hold that thought, Devon, because I want to bring in Chloe from Toronto. Chloe, you've been playing on the Xbox Series S today—how's the game design landing for you?

Chloe

Oh man, I'm kind of torn, Paul. Like, I actually love the mall stuff and the little life-sim details, you know? Going to the gym together, walking along the beach—it makes Leonida feel alive in a way no other game has. But... yeah, Devon's not crazy about the friction. The controls are so heavy and clunky.

Paul

Even The Guardian's five-out-of-five review admitted the controls feel unwieldy and collide with your intentions. Did you run into that?

Chloe

Oh, constantly! There's a viral clip going around PC Gamer today of a player fighting the controls so hard they accidentally bought two slushy shakes and got stuck walking around in a squat chugging them! And that's basically my experience trying to get into cover or pick up loot. You feel like you're wrestling a tank just to turn around in a hallway.

Devon

Thank you! That's what I'm saying! Every simple action takes three extra animations. You want to grab a car and go? Nope, realism check. You want to swap characters? Nope, sometimes the story locks you out of swapping between Jason and Lucia when you're in the middle of a narrative beat.

Paul

Well, let's talk about that relationship mechanic, because that has set X on fire today. Devon, I've seen people complaining that you feel "stuck" in the relationship—specifically, that you can't cheat on Lucia with strippers or side characters like Mandy. Did that bother you, or is that just people looking for something to complain about?

Devon

Look, I don't even care about the strip club thing specifically, Paul, it's what it represents. You can go to a strip club *as a couple*, right? Like, how sanitized is that? In old GTA games, you had total sandbox freedom to be a complete menace. Here, everything is rail-roaded around keeping Jason and Lucia's four-point couple rating intact. Rockstar even put out a statement to Shacknews saying cheating isn't "complementary to the feeling and motivations" of their story. It just feels weirdly tame for a game that used to be gaming's bad boy.

Paul

Let's bring in Hannah from Austin, Texas, who's been studying narrative design and has been deep into the story today. Hannah, is Rockstar right to protect the Bonnie-and-Clyde dynamic, or did they box the player in too much?

Hannah

Honestly, Paul, I think Rockstar made a deliberate creative bet, and people who expected GTA Five part two are getting whiplash. Think about it—the whole spine of GTA Six is Jason and Lucia trying to scrape together enough cash to live crime-free with their son. If the game let you blow up their relationship in free roam on a Tuesday, and then on Wednesday you're supposed to care during a tense bank heist together, the whole emotional arc would collapse.

Chloe

Okay, I get that on paper, Hannah, I really do. And I actually like Lucia—she's tough, she's got real agency, and that prologue where a dye pack can explode in your cash bag if you mess up is super cool. But... can we talk about the dialogue and the voice acting? Because some of it is really rough.

Paul

Really? With a ninety-nine on Metacritic, you're finding the voice acting rough?

Chloe

It's Jason, mostly! Like, I'm sorry, his voice actor sounds so monotone. There's a whole thread on Reddit right now where someone said he sounds like he's got a mouth full of hot soup and the emotional range of a T-1000. In early scenes—like that drug deal scene—he sounds like he's literally reading down at a script, and it kind of drags Lucia's scenes down when she's talking to him.

Hannah

You know, I've been seeing that debate everywhere! Half of X thinks Jason is dull as dishwater, and the other half is pointing out it's an intentional homage to Don Johnson as James Crockett in Miami Vice—that cool, deadpan, introverted foil to Lucia's fiery, extroverted energy.

Devon

Well, homage or not, when I'm nine hours into a game and my main character sounds bored during a shootout, the homage isn't working! And it's not just Jason. Even positive reviews—like CBC News—pointed out that outside of Jason and Lucia, half the supporting cast feels like one-dimensional stereotypes. Twitchy drug runners, lecherous lawyers, over-the-top rap moguls. It's like the tone can't decide if it wants to be a gritty prestige crime drama or a cartoon.

Paul

That is a really fair critique—that tonal tug-of-war between Red Dead Two realism and classic GTA satire. Now, before we get too deep into the writing, we have to talk about the technical state of this game, because that's the other half of X right now. Let's bring in Tariq from Manchester, who's been tracking every frame-rate test and viral bug clip since midnight. Tariq, welcome to the show.

Tariq

Cheers, Paul! Mate, my timeline is an absolute warzone right now. Look, first off, the performance. Every single console is locked to thirty frames per second at launch—no sixty FPS performance mode anywhere, not even on the PS5 Pro.

Paul

Hold on—even the PS5 Pro is thirty FPS?

Tariq

Yep. Digital Foundry's breakdown shows the PS5 Pro is the *only* console that holds a near-locked thirty with PSSR and ray-traced shadows, and even that dips if you go wild with an RPG. Base PS5—what Devon's playing on—regularly drops to twenty-eight or twenty-nine just driving around, and lower in big gunfights. Xbox Series X is mostly thirty, dipping into the mid-twenties during intense sequences, plus there are folks on XboxSupport reporting straight-up crashes on older Series X units.

Chloe

Don't even get me started on the Series S, Tariq! I bought a Series S mostly for this game, and during gameplay it gets this awful, smeary blur. The pop-in is super noticeable, and when things get chaotic, it feels like it drops below twenty frames a second. Cutscenes look gorgeous, and then the camera pulls back to gameplay and it's like someone smeared Vaseline on my TV.

Tariq

Yeah, Series S is averaging around twenty-seven FPS and memory-bound at eight gigs, which is brutal for a world this dense. Honestly, it's impressive the Series S runs the full city density at all, but blurry ray tracing is the price you pay.

Paul

And what about the glitches, Tariq? Because I've seen people comparing this launch to Cyberpunk twenty-seventy-seven, which is the ultimate insult in gaming. Are the bugs really that widespread, or is it just social media magnifying a few weird clips?

Tariq

Oh, it's definitely a mix of both, Paul, but the clips are hilarious! Have you seen the JasonTheWeen clip? He's driving across a bridge and there's a massive boat wedged between the bridge supports and a wall, just thrashing around like a possessed whale! And X user Synaptic_UK posted a truck getting bumped and launching clear across the map like a rocket.

Devon

Wait, the GTA Four swingset glitch is back too, isn't it? I saw someone's car get catapulted into orbit off a random collision!

Tariq

Yeah, Beebom's bug tracker already confirmed the swingset-style physics launch is back! Plus there's clips of Lucia falling straight through the ground under the map during a mission, aircraft clipping right through skyscrapers, and NPCs swimming sideways on dry land. Worse than the funny physics bugs, though, are the progression blockers—people on the Reddit megathread are reporting crashes in the prologue and a nasty softlock in an early Lucia mission right after leaving a bank where the game just refuses to advance.

Hannah

Okay, let's put on our media-literacy hats for one second, though. GameWatcher actually called out how some streamers are jumping on the GTA Six bug bandwagon for clout. Like that viral clip from ohnePixel claiming a mission had a "no fail" state—players checked the footage and realized it was literally just showing the failure outcome!

Paul

That's a great point, Hannah. When a game has nineteen million pre-orders and every streamer on Earth is playing it simultaneously on day one, every single edge-case collision bug gets clipped, uploaded to X, and viewed five million times in an hour.

Tariq

Oh, a hundred percent. Business Standard made the exact same point—millions of players hitting an open world this complex at once will always uncover bugs no QA team could catch. Rockstar already pushed two pre-launch patches on November thirteenth, so patches are coming. Still, after thirteen years and a nearly two-billion-dollar pre-order haul, you can't blame people for expecting a bit more polish—or sixty FPS.

Paul

Let's do a quick whip around the table before we wrap up. Knowing everything we know tonight—the car-theft friction, the locked-in relationship, the divisive voice acting, the thirty FPS ceiling, and the launch-day bugs—is GTA Six actually a bad game, or is it a victim of impossible expectations? Devon, start us off.

Devon

I don't think it's a *bad* game, Paul, the map is insane to look at. But for me, it's a disappointment. They over-engineered the realism and forgot that GTA is supposed to be effortless, chaotic fun. Give me GTA Five's freedom over a four-point couple meter any day.

Chloe

I'm sticking with it! Once they patch the Series S blur and clean up the softlocks, I think the world is genuinely special. I just wish Jason had a bit more energy in the booth and I didn't feel like I was fighting the controller every time I walk into a coffee shop.

Hannah

It's ambitious to a fault. Rockstar tried to build a prestige Bonnie-and-Clyde crime drama inside the most detailed sandbox ever made. That friction is annoying if you just want to steal supercars, sure, but I respect that they didn't just reskin GTA Five.

Tariq

Look, I'm having a blast laughing at the flying trucks on X, and I'll probably double-dip when the PC version eventually drops in eighteen months so I can actually play above thirty FPS. Until then, save often!

Paul

Wise words, Tariq—save often, and maybe watch out for any suspiciously bouncy bridges in Vice City. Huge thanks to Devon in Miami, Chloe in Toronto, Hannah in Austin, and Tariq in Manchester for keeping it real tonight. Whether you're loving the realism or dusting off Saints Row Two, thanks for tuning in. I'm Paul, and we'll see you next time. Goodnight!
